The same park three times over, each pitched at where one of them is now. Every right answer earns a bone, and bones buy hats, coats and park things for the dog, or animals and toys for the house.

Sophia’s parktimes tables to twelve, the division facts that go with them, and multiples of ten. Rory’s parknumber facts to twenty, adding within a hundred, hundreds tens and ones, telling the time, and a first look at tables and sharing. Turbo Karts
What is it?
Kart racing, drawn the way the old 16-bit ones were: a picture of the circuit seen from above, laid back into the screen a band at a time until it becomes a road running to the horizon. The kart drives itself forward — you steer with a thumb, hold DRIFT through the corners to charge a boost, and throw whatever the item box gave you at the kart in front. Eight drivers, three circuits — a sunset beach, a jungle and a volcano — three laps of a long circuit, a single race or a three-round grand prix with points.

Dungeon Dash
What is it?
A dungeon crawl for one thumb. Walk with the left of the screen, your weapon aims and fires itself, and the two buttons on the right are a dash straight through danger and a special the dungeon pays you for killing things. Doors stay shut until the room is clear. Three floors — catacombs, fungal hollow and emberforge — each one ending in a boss the size of the doorway, with shrines, shops, a locked vault and eighteen relics that stack on the way down. Play as Sophia with a longbow, Rory with boomerang blades, or Dylan with fireballs.

Riddle Rumble
What is it?
Wordplay for the boys. Four answers, and a clock that eats the points rather than the turn, so nobody gets cut off mid-think. Two thousand two hundred riddles across a dozen kinds: hidden animals, rhymes, anagrams, sound-alikes, odd ones out, word glue and a few hundred proper head-scratchers. It builds them fresh every time, so nobody learns the answers off by heart. Quick Quiz for twelve and three lives, or Beat the Clock for ninety seconds.

Super Strikers
What is it?
Six-a-side arcade football from straight above, built the way the old 16-bit ones were. The ball is never glued to your feet: you knock it ahead and run onto it, and a tackle is getting your body there first. One thumb steers, one button passes and shoots, and holding a direction after you hit it bends the ball through the air. Eight teams, a quick match, a three-round cup with penalty shoot-outs, and two players on one iPad.

Marble Mayhem
What is it?
A marble run builder. Drag planks, curves, trampolines, bumpers, fans, spinners, cannons and portals onto the board, turn them, press GO and watch the marble roll. Sixty-four puzzles in four worlds, each with a goal cup and bonus stars and a hint that shows one piece at a time when you are stuck, plus six custom sandboxes with a hopper and an end cup you place, marble rain and a longest-run clock for your own contraptions.

What is it?
A first-person spy adventure in three acts across twenty-one countries, from London and Venice through Istanbul, Marrakech, Reykjavik and Singapore to Machu Picchu and the Antarctic ice. Sega Genesis Arcade
What is it?
A real Mega Drive emulator in the browser, with eight free homebrew games on the shelf: platformers, a trap-filled tower puzzle, an egg-catching chicken and a four-player star race. Add cartridges you own to play them on this device. On-screen pad on the iPad.

On an iPad: open a game, then Share → Add to Home Screen for a fullscreen icon. Touch controls turn themselves on automatically. In Agent Rory, the left thumb walks, the right thumb looks, and the big green button investigates whatever is glowing; every mini-game has a HINT button. In Sonic Spin, the left thumb is a floating stick and the right thumb has JUMP (tap again in the air to home in), SPIN, BOOST and DRIFT. In Paws of Fury, the left thumb is the stick, the right thumb has JUMP, BITE and POUND, and dragging the middle of the screen swings the camera. In Lizard Rocket, swipe across a tile to lay an arrow and tap an arrow to pick it up. In Velocity Zero, hold the phone or iPad sideways, drag on the left half of the screen to steer and use the AIRBRAKE and FIRE buttons on the right; in the voxel games, drag the MOVE pad to walk and drag elsewhere to look. In the Genesis Arcade, tap a game and then its Start button; the D-pad and A B C buttons appear on screen, and the bar under the game has fullscreen and save states.
