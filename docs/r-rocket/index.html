Grown-ups

This is for practice between therapy sessions, not instead of them. Practise only the levels his speech therapist says he is ready for: practising an R he can't yet make correctly mostly practises the wrong R. The words leave out R at the end of a word (car, four), which a British accent doesn't sound anyway.

1 · Set the rocket to Rory's voice

The rocket watches the third resonance of his voice (F3). A good R pulls it well down; an "ah" or a "w"-ish R leaves it high. Everyone's voice is different, so measure his.

Ask him to hold the sound for three seconds, about a hand's length from the iPad. Use best R after his therapist has helped him make a good one: that is the best setting.

2 · Target line

The rocket climbs when F3 is below this line. Lower makes it harder.

3 · How he's doing

Words scored by you, by day and level. Many therapists look for about 80% correct before moving up a level.

What the rocket can and can't tell

It measures one thing reliably on a held sound: whether F3 has come down, which is what a good R does. It can't judge a whole word. In a quick word the R lasts a few hundredths of a second, and a phone's speech recogniser would "hear" wabbit as rabbit anyway. So in Words you are the judge, and the rocket's dip shows only as a hint. Recordings stay on this iPad and are not kept.
