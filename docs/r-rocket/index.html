Mission Control

For practice between therapy sessions, not instead of them. Practise only the levels his speech therapist says he is ready for: practising an R he can't make yet mostly practises the wrong one. The words leave out R at the end of a word (car, four), which a British accent doesn't sound anyway.

1 · Set the rocket to Rory's voice

Everything listens to the third resonance of his voice (F3). A good R pulls it well down; an "ah" or a "w"-ish R leaves it high. Voices differ, so measure his.

Ask him to hold the sound for four seconds, about a hand's length from the iPad. Best R is the better setting: measure it right after his therapist has helped him make a good one.

2 · Target line (Star Run)

The rocket climbs when F3 is below this line. Lower is harder.

3 · Who judges the words?

4 · How he's doing

Words by day and level (the final call on each word, after any correction). Many therapists look for about 80% before moving up a level.

What the judge can and can't hear

It listens for one thing: whether F3 dips low enough, for long enough, somewhere in the word, which is what a good R does and a "w"-like R doesn't. It can't tell whether he said the right word, or judge any other sound. It will sometimes be wrong, so it says "not sure" rather than guess, and every call can be corrected. A wrong "Great R!" teaches the wrong R, so it is set to be stingy with them, and it gets better at his voice from your corrections. Recordings stay on this iPad and are not kept.
